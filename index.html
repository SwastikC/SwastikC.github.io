About

Trained at the Indian Institute of Astrophysics, with stops at ESO Santiago and the University of Hyderabad, I now work at the Università degli Studi di Milano on exoplanetary science and stellar astrophysics.

My doctoral work focused on the chemical and kinematic fingerprints of exoplanet host stars — how metallicity, galactic origin, and age conspire to produce the planets we observe. More recently I've moved into high-contrast imaging, modeling protoplanetary disks observed with VLT/SPHERE.

Outside research, I mentor masters students, run sky-watch programs for under-resourced schools in and around Bangalore (supported by the IAU's Open Astronomy Schools project), and volunteer with animal-rights groups in India.

Research

I work at the intersection of stellar astrophysics and exoplanetary science. My research themes:

Exoplanet host-star chemistry

How the metallicity and detailed chemical composition of stars dictate which planets can form around them, and what that tells us about the planet population we observe.

Stellar ages & galactic context

Combining isochrone fitting, kinematics from GAIA DR3, and chemical abundance proxies to date exoplanet host stars and place them in galactic chemical evolution.

Protoplanetary disk imaging

High-contrast imaging and polarimetric differential imaging of disks around young stars with VLT/SPHERE — directly observing the environments where planets are born.

Brown-dwarf desert

Investigating why so few brown dwarfs orbit close to sun-like stars by characterising the host stars of directly-imaged brown-dwarf companions.

Research Highlights

A visual tour of key findings. Hover or tap a card to read more.

2021 · AJ 161, 114

DIP host stars span a wide metallicity range

Mean [Fe/H] for 22 directly-imaged planet hosts is −0.04±0.27 dex — close to solar with large scatter. This suggests massive wide-orbit planets don't require metal-rich environments, and may form via gravitational instability rather than core accretion.

C. Swastik et al.
2022 · AJ 164, 60

High-mass planetary systems are young

Combining stellar ages with planet-mass distributions across the galaxy: the most massive planetary systems orbit systematically younger stars, suggesting their formation depends on conditions that have evolved over cosmic time.

C. Swastik et al.
2023 · AJ 166, 91

Dating exoplanet hosts with GAIA DR3

Combining chemical-clock proxies with GAIA-derived kinematics to estimate ages for 1000+ exoplanet host stars — the largest such sample to date.

C. Swastik et al.
In review · A&A

Imaging the LkCa 15 disk without artefacts

A new pipeline for polarimetric differential imaging with VLT/SPHERE produces a clean, artefact-free view of the LkCa 15 protoplanetary disk in both total intensity and polarized light — revealing structure where planets may be forming.

C. Swastik, Z. Wahhaj, et al.

How I work

The tools, pipelines, and habits behind my research.

Spectroscopic reduction

Reducing high-resolution stellar spectra from HCT/HFOSC and other facilities to derive precise stellar parameters and chemical abundances.

  • iSpec
  • IRAF
  • PyRAF
  • MOOG
  • Astropy

High-contrast imaging

Polarimetric and total-intensity reduction of VLT/SPHERE data to image protoplanetary disks at sub-arcsecond scales.

  • IRDAP
  • SPHERE DC
  • PDI pipelines
  • RADMC-3D

Statistical inference

Bayesian parameter estimation for stellar ages, masses, and orbital fits — most often with MCMC sampling.

  • emcee
  • NumPyro
  • scipy
  • isochrones

Reproducible workflows

I version-control everything, write modular Python code, and document analyses in Jupyter notebooks I share with collaborators.

  • Python
  • Jupyter
  • Git
  • pandas
  • matplotlib
