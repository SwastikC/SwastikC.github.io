About

Dr. Swastik Chowbay — postdoctoral fellow at the Università degli Studi di Milano, funded under the ERC Starting Grant UNVEIL with PI Stefano Facchini. Working on the boundary between exoplanetary science and stellar astrophysics.

Dr. Swastik's doctoral work — at the Indian Institute of Astrophysics under Ravinder Banyal — focused on the chemical and kinematic fingerprints of exoplanet host stars: how metallicity, galactic origin, and age conspire to produce the planets we observe. The thesis, Planet formation: From protoplanets to the main sequence, spans that whole range.

In Milan he leads high-contrast imaging programs on VLT/SPHERE (star-hopping technique, polarimetric imaging of disks like LkCa 15 and the upcoming HD 61005 "Moth"), and contributes as Co-I on a JWST Cycle 5 program targeting molecular H₂ in a protoplanetary disk. He also referees for AJ and A&A, mentors master's students, and runs sky-watch programs for under-resourced schools in India.

Research

I work at the intersection of stellar astrophysics and exoplanetary science. My research themes:

Star-hopping & high-contrast imaging

Imaging protoplanetary disks and embedded planets with VLT/SPHERE (star-hopping technique) and JWST. The LkCa 15 reduction achieves ~2 mag contrast improvement over classical ADI/RDI.

Exoplanet host-star chemistry

How the metallicity and detailed chemical composition of stars dictate which planets can form. Wide-orbit directly-imaged giants do not require metal-rich hosts — pointing to gravitational instability.

Stellar ages & galactic context

Combining isochrone fitting, GAIA DR3 kinematics, and chemical age proxies to date exoplanet host stars. High-mass-planet hosts are systematically younger — galactic chemical evolution shapes demographics.

Accreting protoplanets

First optical–NIR spectroscopic baselines of newly imaged accreting protoplanets (WISPIT-2 campaign). PI on HCT time and the CINECA HPC allocation for follow-up modeling.

Research Highlights

2026 · A&A in press

LkCa 15 imaged without self-subtraction artefacts

Star-hopping with VLT/SPHERE produces a clean polarimetric + total-intensity view of the LkCa 15 protoplanetary disk — ~2 mag contrast improvement over classical ADI/RDI methods, revealing structure where planets may be forming.

C. Swastik, Z. Wahhaj, et al.
2021 · AJ 161, 114

DIP hosts span a wide metallicity range

Mean [Fe/H] for 22 directly-imaged planet hosts: −0.04±0.27 dex — close to solar with large scatter. Wide-orbit massive planets don't require metal-rich environments, suggesting a formation pathway distinct from close-in giants (likely gravitational instability).

C. Swastik et al.
2022 · AJ 164, 60

Galactic chemical evolution shapes planet demographics

Combining stellar ages with planet-mass distributions across the galaxy: high-mass planetary systems orbit systematically younger stars. Planet demographics are partly a fossil record of where in the galaxy — and when — a star was born.

C. Swastik et al.
2023 · AJ 166, 91

Dating exoplanet hosts with GAIA DR3

Combining chemical-clock proxies with GAIA-derived kinematics to estimate ages for 1000+ exoplanet host stars — the largest such sample to date, anchoring the age–mass story.

C. Swastik et al.
